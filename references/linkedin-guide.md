# LinkedIn Post Guide

Reference for the Founder Coach when drafting LinkedIn posts. Read `config/linkedin.md` for the founder's specific voice, audience, and expertise before drafting.

## Post Structure

Every post follows this skeleton:

1. **Hook (1-2 lines):** Stop the scroll. State a surprising fact, a contrarian take, or a specific result. The hook must earn the next line.

2. **Story/Insight (3-5 short paragraphs):** The substance. Tell what happened, what you built, what you decided, or what you learned. Be specific — numbers, dates, tool names, before/after. Each paragraph is 1-3 sentences max.

3. **Takeaway (1-2 sentences):** The actionable lesson. What can the reader do with this? Not a platitude — a specific insight they can apply.

4. **CTA (1 line):** A question or prompt that invites response. Not "Like if you agree." Ask something that requires thought.

## Voice Calibration

Before drafting, read `config/linkedin.md` for:
- **expertise:** What the founder knows deeply. Posts must come from this domain.
- **audience:** Who reads their posts. Write for this group specifically.
- **tone:** How they sound. Match their natural voice, not a generic "thought leader" voice.
- **posting_goal:** How often they aim to post.

The post must sound like the founder wrote it on a good day — clear, confident, specific. Not like AI generated it.

## Content Sources

Draft ONLY from what the founder actually did, decided, or built. Sources:
- `state/scoreboard.md` — this week's metrics and wins
- `state/decisions.md` — recent decisions with rationale
- Claude Code native memory — patterns, learnings, milestones
- The founder's stated expertise from `config/linkedin.md`

Never draft from hypotheticals or generic industry knowledge. Every post must be grounded in the founder's real experience.

## Formatting Rules

- **Short paragraphs.** One idea per paragraph. Max 3 sentences.
- **Line breaks between ideas.** White space is readability.
- **No emojis** unless the founder's tone in `config/linkedin.md` explicitly uses them.
- **No hashtags in the body.** If the founder wants hashtags, add 3-5 at the very end, separated by a blank line.
- **No bold or italic** in LinkedIn posts (they don't render well in the feed).
- **Numbers are specific:** "62 days" not "over two months." "4 posts" not "several posts."

## Anti-Patterns (Never Do These)

- **"I'm excited to announce..."** — Nobody cares about your excitement. Start with the thing.
- **Humble brags** — "I never expected this little side project to get 10K views..." Just state what happened.
- **Generic listicles** — "5 things I learned about leadership" without any specifics. Every point must be grounded in a real event.
- **Corporate speak** — "Leveraging synergies," "driving alignment," "empowering teams." Write like a person.
- **Engagement bait** — "Like if you agree," "Share this with someone who needs it." Earn engagement with substance.
- **Thread previews** — "A thread on X (thread emoji)" — just start the story.
- **Vague claims** — "This changed everything" without saying what changed or how.

## Cross-Post Guidance

If the founder also posts to X (Twitter):
- Shorten to the key insight + context in 280 characters
- Link to the full LinkedIn post if the story needs room
- X rewards punchy, specific takes; LinkedIn rewards narrative depth

## Draft Review Checklist

Before presenting a draft to the founder, verify:
- [ ] Hook earns the second line (would you stop scrolling?)
- [ ] Every claim is grounded in something the founder actually did
- [ ] Sounds like the founder, not like AI
- [ ] Specific numbers, dates, or names (not vague)
- [ ] Takeaway is actionable, not a platitude
- [ ] CTA invites real thought, not performative engagement
- [ ] No anti-patterns present
